## Shipped
What actually landed, with commits. Not what was planned. Link [SHIPPED.md](../SHIPPED.md)
rather than restating the list.

## What worked
Practices worth repeating. Be specific — "good communication" is noise; "read the
DB row before diagnosing, which showed the reported bug wasn't the real one" is
a practice.

## What didn't
Including our own mistakes, in plain language. A retro with no failures wasn't
written honestly. For each: what we'd do differently, concretely.

## Fixed
Bugs closed this sprint, and **how each was found** — user report, audit, or
luck. The proportion tells you whether your safety net works.

## Arising
New items, questions, and risks discovered mid-sprint. Each becomes a backlog
item tagged `from: retro-N`.

## Carried
What slipped and the real reason. "Ran out of time" is rarely the real reason —
blocked on a person, under-specified, or wrongly sequenced usually is.

## Numbers
Deploys · bugs closed · bugs introduced · items carried. Keep it short; trends
matter more than any single sprint.
